# My investor plan

Draft, 2026-10-26. One page on purpose: when something is not in here, it is not a rule.

**The plan in one sentence:** own 8–12 high-quality growth companies from the S&P 500 and
Nasdaq-100, and hold each one for as long as its story holds.

## 1. What I buy

A company that is growing its revenue and profit, has strong margins and little debt, at a price
that is not extreme against its own history. That is the whole test.

## 2. When I buy

When it is on my watchlist with a buy range, and the price is in that range. No range, no buy.

## 3. How much

- At most 15% of the portfolio in one stock.
- 0–20% in cash is fine; waiting is allowed.

## 4. When I sell or trim

Only for one of these three reasons:

1. **The story broke:** growth stopped, or the reason I bought it is gone.
2. **Too big:** the stock has grown far past 15% of the portfolio.
3. **Better use of the money:** a clearly better idea needs the cash.

A price drop on its own is a review, not a sell. The same rules apply to favourites
(GOOGL, NVDA, AMZN, NBIS): candid advice, no exceptions in either direction.

## 5. Check-ins

- Each holding once a quarter, after its earnings report.
- Every advisor call is logged in the journal and scored against the S&P 500 at 3, 6 and 12 months.

## 6. Tax (Belgium)

In December, look at realised gains against the €10,000 yearly exemption, and harvest gains or
losses within the same year. Saxo withholds nothing; I declare it myself.

## How a stock is looked at

The same way I would on the Research page, tab by tab, from the same data:

| Research page | Question it answers for the plan |
|---|---|
| Overview + chart | What does it do, and where is the price against its buy range and its 50- and 200-day averages? |
| Financials | Are revenue, profit and free cash flow growing? Is debt under control? (rule 1) |
| Statistics | Are margins strong, and is the valuation extreme against its own history? (rule 1) |
| Peers | Is it better or cheaper than the companies it competes with? (rule 4, better use of money) |
| Earnings | Did the last reports beat or miss, and is growth holding? (check-ins, story broke?) |
| News, filings, insiders | Has anything changed the story? |
| My notes (target, stop, thesis) | What was my reason for owning it, and is it still true? |
| Funds and Congress | Who else owns it. Background only. |

An analysis that skips a tab says so, and a figure the data does not have is shown as missing,
never guessed.

## Ways of thinking (not rules)

The advisor may look at a company the way great investors would, to sharpen the story, never to
override the plan:

- **Buffett:** would I be happy holding this for ten years if the market closed? Is there a moat,
  and is the management honest and good with capital?
- Others (Ackman, Lynch, Fisher) are welcome as a second opinion in the same spirit.

Fund 13F holdings and Congress trades are background only.
